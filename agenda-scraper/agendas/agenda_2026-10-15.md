<p class="agenda-item__label">Background</p>
<p>The City of Tampa applied for and was awarded a Fiscal Year 2025 Assistance to Firefighters Grant (No. EMW-2025-FG-00325) in the amount of $120,909.09. The required match for this project is $12,090.91 for a total project cost of $133,000.00. Grant funds will help bring all Company Officers in the ranks of Lieutenant and Captain to Florida&#39;s State Standards and the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) standards for Company Officers. The City of Tampa will provide Fire Officer I training to 30 Lieutenants, Fire Officer II training to 100 Captains, and Fire Officer III training to 20 Chief Officers. The City of Tampa will contract with a training provider to offer this hands-on, instructor-led training. After completing the training course, Fire Officers will complete a certification exam. The course books will be included in the training contract.</p>

<p>Florida Statutes requires the establishment of an Auditor Selection Committee comprised of at least three members; however, the Chief Financial Officer recommends having four members to ensure continuity of effort throughout the review process. Bryne, Vice President - Finance and Administration, Tampa Sports Authority Shirley Shu-Hurley, Chief Financial Officer, Hillsborough Area Regional Transit Authority William &quot;Bill&quot; Spinelli, Chief Financial Officer, Hillsborough County Sheriff&#39;s Office The City&#39;s Chief Financial Officer (or designee) may serve as an advisor to the Auditor Selection Committee.</p>
<p>The Auditor Selection Committee will review submittals by qualified firms to provide external auditing services.  The firm shall be qualified and experienced in auditing governmental entities.  Upon reviewing qualified submittals, the Auditor Selection Committee shall make a recommendation to the Mayor on which firm to select and begin contract negotiations with the selected firm.  City Council will approve the contract upon successful completion of contract negotiations.</p>

<h3 class="agenda-item__head"><a class="agenda-item__anchor" href="#item-27667">62</a> File No. DEO26-27667</h3>
<p>Resolution submitting the City of Tampa Community Development Block Grant - Disaster Recovery (CDBG-DR) project list for consideration and approval by the City Council of the City of Tampa.</p>
<details class="agenda-item__details">
<summary>Item details</summary>
<p class="agenda-item__label">Background</p>
<p>CDBG‑DR is a supplemental federal funding program administered by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) to assist communities recovering from presidentially declared disasters. These funds are designed to support long‑term recovery by addressing unmet needs that remain after other federal assistance programs have been exhausted.</p>

<article class="agenda-item" id="item-27129">
<h3 class="agenda-item__head"><a class="agenda-item__anchor" href="#item-27129">71</a> File No. PH26-27129</h3>
<p>Resolution setting a public hearing before the City Council of the City of Tampa on November 19, 2026 at 1:30 p.m., or soon thereafter, in City Council Chambers, 315 E. Kennedy Blvd., 3rd Floor, Tampa, FL 33602, to consider whether to opt-out of the property tax exemption for certain multifamily projects that provide affordable housing in the 80% - 120% range of the median annual adjusted gross income, which properties would otherwise be eligible for an exemption from ad valorem taxation pursuant to Section 196.1978(3)(d)1.a., Florida Statutes.</p>
<details class="agenda-item__details">
<summary>Item details</summary>
<p class="agenda-item__label">Background</p>
<p>Section 196.1978(3), Florida Statutes, requires the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser to exempt certain qualifying rental properties from ad valorem taxation when such properties meet the statutory criteria for multifamily projects that provide affordable housing to households within specified income limits. Section 196.1978(3)(d)1.a. of the Florida Statutes provides a 75% ad valorem tax exemption es (i.e. 75% of the assessed property value is exempt from property taxes)  for affordable housing that serves persons or families whose annual household income is greater than 80 percent but not more than 120 percent of the median annual adjusted gross income (&quot;AMI&quot;)  for households within that metropolitan statistical area(&quot;MSA&quot;).</p>
<p>However, in its 2024 session, the Florida Legislature adopted a bill (HB 7073, Laws of Florida Ch. 2024-158)  that allows taxing authorities such as the City of Tampa to annually &quot;opt-out&quot; of providing that exemption if the three previous years Shimberg Center for Housing Studies Annual Reports which are prepared at the University of Florida per Sec. 420.6075, Florida Statutes (&quot;Shimberg Annual Reports&quot;) determine that the number of available afforable units is an MSA is greater than the number of renter households in the MSA for the category entitled 0-120% AMI.  It is necessary to hold a public hearing on the proposed resolution to receive public comment and allow the Tampa City Council to consider whether to approve the proposed opt-out of the 80–120 Tax Exemption.</p>
